02 | Your Information

Your Information Has Value

Everything you post publicly can be collected, combined, and used. Not just by people with bad intentions, but by automated tools that scrape public data without anyone manually looking.

Pay attention to the background. It may reveal more information than you intend.

The circles in this image highlight what a visitor could read without permission: what is on screen, notes on the wall, documents left in plain sight. The same instinct applies to what you post publicly online.

A single post may seem harmless. Combined with other public information, it can form a surprisingly complete picture of who you are, where you are, and how to approach you convincingly.

04 | Be Mindful

What to Watch For

Small adjustments to what you share can make a real difference. Explore each area below.

The background of a photo can reveal your home address, daily route, vehicle, or workplace without you realizing it. Street signs, license plates, house numbers, and office windows all carry information.

Risky

A photo outside your home with the house number, street sign, or parked vehicle visible.

Safer

Crop or blur identifying details before posting. Post after you leave a location, not while you are there.

People in your photos can become targets. A message referencing your child by name, or your partner's face, can be used to lower your guard in ways a generic message never could.

Risky

Full names, faces, and relationships publicly visible to anyone, not just your connections.

Safer

Share the moment without the identifying details. Keep profiles private where possible.

The specific words you use matter more than you might think. Names, nicknames, and personal details in your posts can be used in two ways: to make targeted messages more convincing, and as clues to passwords.

Risky

"In the plaza with Bobby" reveals a name, a location, and who you trust. Pet names and partner names are also common password ingredients.

Safer

"Out with a friend" shares the same moment without the details that could be used against you.

05 | How Are You Doing?

A Quick Check-In

No scores, no judgement. Answer honestly and we will show you only the tips that are relevant to you.

  • Do you use the same password for more than one account?
  • Is your main social media profile set to public?
  • Do you have two-step verification turned on for your important accounts?
  • Do you post your location while you are still there?
  • When did you last check your privacy settings?

On reusing passwords

Remembering one password is hard enough. Remembering twenty different ones is unrealistic, so reusing them is understandable. The problem: when one site gets breached, attackers automatically try that same email and password on every other site. It's called credential stuffing, and it's how one leak becomes five.

The easiest fix: a password manager. Apps like Bitwarden (free) or 1Password create and remember a different strong password for every account. You only remember one.

Not ready for one yet? Use a random passphrase. Four or more unrelated words are long, easy to remember, and hard to guess:

velvet-canoe-orbit-pickle

Use a different one for each important account, starting with your email.

Why not one base password with the site name added, like Summer2024_Instagram? Because if one version leaks, the pattern gives away all the others.

On public profiles

A public profile means anyone, not just people you know, can see your posts, your photos, your connections, and your location history. Most platforms make it straightforward to switch to a more private setting without losing your content.

Check your settings today. Look for "Privacy" or "Audience" in your account settings and set your default audience to Friends or Connections only.

On two-step verification

Two-step verification means that even if someone gets your password, they still cannot get into your account without a second confirmation, usually a code sent to your phone.

It takes about two minutes to set up and is one of the most effective things you can do. Look for "Two-step verification" or "Two-factor authentication" in the security settings of your email and any accounts that matter to you.

On posting your location in real time

Posting while you are somewhere tells anyone watching exactly where you are right now, and by extension, where you are not. Your home, your office, your usual route.

A simple habit: post the photo after you have left. You still share the moment, and nobody knows your current whereabouts.

On checking privacy settings

Platforms update their privacy settings regularly, sometimes in ways that reset your preferences or introduce new options. A quick check every few months takes less than five minutes and keeps you in control of who sees what.

Set a reminder once a quarter. Five minutes now can prevent a lot of unwanted exposure later.

You are in good shape! Keep doing what you are doing, and share these tips with the people around you.
