What does a website actually see when you visit it?

Every time you open a tab, websites can collect information about your device, browser, and behaviour without ever asking for your name or password. This activity runs the same collection techniques on your own browser so you can see exactly what is exposed. Nothing leaves your device. Everything you see is generated locally and disappears when you close the tab.

Device Fingerprinting
Your browser, screen, fonts, and hardware form a fingerprint that identifies you across sites even without cookies.
Network Exposure
Your IP address and sometimes your local network address can be detected, narrowing your approximate location.
Behavioural Tracking
Sites track where you click, when you switch tabs, where your cursor moves, and how long you stay.
Passive Signals
Battery level, connected devices, timezone, and language are readable without any permission prompt.
How device fingerprinting works Every browser renders audio, graphics, and fonts slightly differently depending on the device hardware and software. This activity generates a hash from your audio processor output, canvas rendering, installed fonts, and CPU performance. Combined, those values are unique enough to identify your device across websites without cookies. Clearing your browser history does not change this fingerprint.
Try this during the session. After clicking Start, switch to another tab or browse somewhere else, then come back. Real websites collect data while you are gone and log exactly when you left and returned. This activity does the same so you can see it in real time. Note: keystrokes in other apps or tabs are protected by your operating system and cannot be captured here. That boundary is itself an important security concept.
What to capture for your portfolio:
  1. The Device and Network panel once values have populated.
  2. The Activity Log and cursor heatmap after switching tabs at least once.
  3. The Fingerprint panel showing your audio, canvas, and font values.
